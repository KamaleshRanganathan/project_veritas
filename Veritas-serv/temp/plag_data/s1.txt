The Solar System: An Overview

The Solar System is the gravitationally bound system comprising the Sun and the objects that orbit it. Formed approximately 4.6 billion years ago from the collapse of a giant molecular cloud, it consists of one star—the Sun—and eight planets, along with numerous moons, asteroids, comets, and dwarf planets.

At the center lies the Sun, a G-type main-sequence star that accounts for 99.86% of the system's total mass. It provides the energy that drives weather, climate, and life on Earth through nuclear fusion in its core.

The eight planets are divided into two groups. The inner terrestrial planets—Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars—are rocky worlds with solid surfaces. Earth, the third planet, is the only known body to harbor life, thanks to its liquid water and protective atmosphere. The outer gas giants—Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune—are massive planets primarily composed of hydrogen and helium. Jupiter, the largest, has a mass 318 times that of Earth and features the famous Great Red Spot, a storm persisting for centuries.

Beyond Neptune lies the Kuiper Belt, a region of icy bodies including Pluto, now classified as a dwarf planet. Further out is the scattered disc and the hypothetical Oort Cloud, the source of long-period comets.

The Solar System also hosts natural satellites. Earth has one large moon, while Mars has two small ones—Phobos and Deimos. The gas giants possess extensive moon systems; Saturn alone has over 80 known moons, including Titan with its thick atmosphere.

Asteroids, mostly found in the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter, are remnants from the Solar System's formation. Comets, originating from the Kuiper Belt or Oort Cloud, display bright tails when nearing the Sun due to sublimation of ices.

Understanding the Solar System helps us grasp planetary formation, the potential for extraterrestrial life, and the future evolution of our star. Ongoing missions like NASA's Perseverance rover and the James Webb Space Telescope continue to expand our knowledge.
